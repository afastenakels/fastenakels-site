Read this first — the 2025 "collapse" is a booking break, not a business collapse. Total receipts drop from 208.7 Bn (2024) to 176.5 Bn (2025), but the entire fall sits in the prepaid Cash Power line, which falls from ~5.5–6 Bn/month to ~0.6 Bn/month from August 2025 while its number of ledger entries stays constant. Postpaid LV and MV receipts are stable through year-end. Prepaid sales almost certainly moved to a channel that no longer posts through these tills. Before using 2025 totals anywhere, get the missing prepaid channel data from CEET.

01The shape of the cash machine

This is receipts, not billing: what customers actually paid, month by month. Three engines — postpaid LV bills, prepaid Cash Power vending, and 985-odd MV accounts — plus a small works/connections line.

Monthly cash receipts by engine, 60 months

Bn FCFA per month. The prepaid break in August 2025 is unmistakable.

Prepaid was the fastest-growing engine until the break — from ~3 Bn/month in 2021 to ~6 Bn/month by mid-2025. MV receipts are remarkably flat at 4.3–5.9 Bn/month across the whole five years.

Annual receipts by engine

Bn FCFA. Prepaid + postpaid + MV + works.

Prepaid grew 64% in four years (37.0 → 60.7 Bn, 2021→2024) while postpaid LV grew 39%. On trend, prepaid would have passed 40% of LV cash in 2026.

Who pays: receipts by customer category

Bn FCFA, five-year totals, ledger categories.

Private customers put in 77% of all cash (688 Bn). The whole public sector — state companies, administration, agencies, local authorities — paid 123.7 Bn (13.9%), less than its share of billing, which is exactly the arrears story in the datapack.

02How Togo pays its electricity bill

The settlement ledger records a payment mode on every one of the 13.6M receipts — a picture of payment behaviour the summary datapack cannot see.

Payment modes by value

Bn FCFA per year. ES = cash at till, CH = cheque, AC = credit/offset, EB = electronic, VI = bank transfer, PT = phone/mobile.

Cash at the till is still 46% of all value and 64% of all transactions. Cheques are 27% of value on only 1.7% of transactions — that is the corporate/state channel.

Digital payments: counts vs value

EB + PT (electronic + phone) share of transactions and of value.

A third of all payment events are already digital, but under 10% of the money. Digital is used for small tickets (avg ~18k FCFA vs 1M+ for cheques). PT (phone) tripled its transaction count from 65k (2022) to 172k (2025).

LV bill-payment ticket sizes

Postpaid LV energy receipts (piece FE), 10.8M records, five years pooled.

55% of receipts are under 25,000 FCFA, but 49% of the money comes from receipts over 100k — the LV book is a mass of tiny payments carried by a thin commercial top end.

Payment discipline: months active per payer

Of ~400k LV references that paid in 2024, how many months did each appear?

The base is split in two: 171k references touched the tills only once in 2024 (mostly prepaid buyers and one-off works), while a disciplined core of ~117k paid in 10+ months — the true monthly-bill population.

03Concentration and the MV cash base

The receipts ledger confirms the datapack's concentration warning, with names and numbers.

Top MV cash contributors, 2021–2025

Bn FCFA of receipts. Cement, port and industry dominate.

CIMTOGO alone paid 18.3 Bn; the three cement groups (CIMTOGO + CIMCO + Diamond + Fortia + CIMTOGO Kara) together ~47 Bn — 16% of all MV cash. KALYAN, the datapack's arrears flag, still paid 5.4 Bn.

MV concentration curve

Share of five-year MV cash by count of accounts.

36 accounts deliver half of all MV cash; 213 deliver 80%. The remaining ~950 MV references contribute a fifth of the money — the receivable risk is a short, negotiable list.

Payers by voltage class

Receipts by agency (code), 2024

Bn FCFA. First two digits of the customer reference = agency.

Agency 10 books a third of everything — it carries the bulk Cash Power vending entries and the head-office cheques. The Lomé agencies (01–06, 12) plus 10 are ~85% of national cash.

Cash seasonality (2021–2024 average)

Bn FCFA by calendar month, prepaid break year excluded.

Cash peaks in March and September — receipts follow the quarterly billing/dunning cycle more than consumption weather. The consumption seasonality in the datapack (April peak) barely shows in cash.

04Churn: the termination register

81,340 terminated accounts, with creation and termination dates — a subscriber-lifecycle view that exists nowhere in the summary datapack.

Terminations per year

Accounts closed, by year of termination.

Churn has doubled since 2022 (2,752 → 5,078). Against ~920k active accounts that is still only ~0.55%/year — but the trend line is the thing to watch, and it turned up exactly when the arrears crisis deepened.

Lifetime of terminated accounts

Years from creation to termination, accounts closed since 2015.

Median life of a closed account: 6.5 years; 8% die within a year. Those early deaths are connection-fee revenue that never became a customer — worth a look for the acquisition process.

05Arrears & portfolio quality

A receivables view rebuilt from payment behaviour: since the ledger records when every account last paid and how often, the health of the postpaid book can be aged and tracked even without an invoice file.

Payment-silence aging of the postpaid book

237k postpaid LV accounts (≥3 bill payments since 2023), by months since their last payment, as of Dec 2025.

82.3% of the book is current; 17.7% (42k accounts) has gone quiet for 2+ months. The dangerous part: accounts silent 2–6 months carry average tickets of 61–80k FCFA — two to three times the book average of 29k. It is the bigger accounts that go quiet.

Where accounts go: discipline transitions 2024 → 2025

Each 2024 discipline bucket traced into 2025. Regular = paid 10–12 months, Semi = 5–9, Weak = 1–4.

The private core is stable: only 2.2% of regular payers deteriorate to weak or silent. But weak payers are a trap door — 72% of them stay weak or vanish. Nearly identical to the 2021→2022 matrix, so the arrears crisis has not (yet) degraded private discipline.

Implied new arrears per year (billed − energy cash)

Bn FCFA. Billing from the Elyos datapack; cash from this ledger. Negative = catch-up year.

Arrears build in a two-year cycle — a bad year (2023: +17.7 Bn combined, 2025: +25.1 Bn) followed by a partial catch-up (2024: +0.9 Bn net). That is the state settling in bursts, not customers gradually slipping. 2025's LV gap carries the usual booking-break caveat.

Public-sector monthly receipts: the burst pattern

Bn FCFA per month from state categories, 60 months.

Public receipts swing from 0.6 to 8.9 Bn/month (volatility 5× private). Half a year's state cash can arrive in two months. This is why the arrears stock saw-tooths — and why a budget-escrow mechanism matters more than dunning.

Catch-up payments (2+ bills settled at once)

Share of postpaid account-months settling multiple bills in one visit.

Steady at ~5.5–6% since 2022 (2021's 11.2% was the COVID backlog clearing). Roughly 250k bills a year are paid late-then-together — the measurable "late but recovered" slice of the book.

Recoveries from disconnected accounts

Bill payments made while the meter was already removed (état compteur D).

Post-disconnection recoveries fell from 2.1 Bn (2021) to 0.25 Bn (2025) — consistent with the datapack's rising disconnection execution rate (82.1% in 2025): debts are being cut earlier, before they grow, leaving less to chase afterwards.

What to ask CEET for next. (1) The post-July-2025 prepaid vending data — without it every 2025 total is understated by ~5 Bn/month. (2) The billing (invoice) file that pairs with this settlement file, so payment lag per account can be computed. (3) A mapping of MODE_REGL codes and of agency codes to names, to firm up the channel and geography reads.
