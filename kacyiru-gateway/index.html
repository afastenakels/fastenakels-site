Chapter One

A corner the city grew around

Stand on the RN3 in Kacyiru in the early morning and you can watch a capital go to work. Ministry staff and diplomats, UN agency teams, consultants landing from three continents. The President's Office is up the hill. The flags of embassies line the road. In one direction, ten minutes away, the towers of the CBD. In the other, five minutes, the dome of the Kigali Convention Centre catching the sun.

In the middle of all this sits 7,853 square metres of land: flat, titled, zoned C3 city-commercial under the Kigali Master Plan, with full frontage on the national road, right beside the Embassy of Egypt. Land like this almost never comes to market in Kacyiru. It has now, at $3–4 million.

Everything a great building needs is already there. The demand walks past every day. Only the building is missing.

“The demand already walks past the site every morning. Only the building is missing.”
Chapter Two

The city that decided to matter

Kigali did not become one of Africa's most watched capitals by accident. It was decided. A convention centre was built before the demand existed, and the demand came. Visas were opened, direct flights multiplied, and the summits followed: heads of state, global health, finance, technology. The international hotel groups noticed, and kept building.

Around the conference calendar grew something more durable: a permanent community of diplomats, development professionals and firms that need to be next to government. They need somewhere to stay for three months, not three nights. They need offices worthy of a headquarters. Kacyiru, their district, has surprisingly little of either.

Chapter Three

One address, four engines

Kacyiru Gateway is a single landmark of roughly 21,400 square metres designed around the people who already live their working lives on this corridor. Not another hotel competing with the global brands, but the things the district visibly lacks, combined under one roof so that each strengthens the others: residences fill the restaurants, offices fill the parking by day, events fill the residences.

90 keys

Serviced residences

Upscale extended-stay suites for diplomatic rotations, development postings and corporate missions that last weeks or months. The district's most under-served need.

Chapter Four

Numbers that tell the same story

The economics rest on a simple spread: we build income at a cost the market no longer offers, then own an asset the market prices dearly. Every assumption behind these figures sits in an open financial model that partners can stress-test line by line, and each one is deliberately set below the top of its market range.
