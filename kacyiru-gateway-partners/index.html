The deal, in plain numbers.

I found a rare piece of land on the main road of Kigali's diplomatic district, next to the Embassy of Egypt. This page explains the business case as simply as I can, so we can decide together whether to go for it. The decision on the table today costs us $50k: securing the land before anyone else does.

In one view

Buy the corner. Build what's missing. Own the income.

Kacyiru is where Rwanda governs: ministries, embassies, UN agencies, the President's Office. The district is full of well-paid professionals and international visitors, yet it has almost no quality long-stay accommodation and little modern office space. On its main road, 7,853 sqm of commercially-zoned land is for sale at $3–4m.

The plan: build one landmark with four income streams: 90 serviced residences for diplomats and consultants on long missions, ~7,900 sqm of offices, shops and restaurants at street level, and event space five minutes from the Convention Centre. The money to build it comes from outside investors and a bank, which we raise once the deal is verified. Not from us.

3. That income makes it worth ~$43.6m

Buyers of income-producing buildings in Kigali pay roughly 12 times annual net income for prime assets. Build for $31.2m, own something worth $43.6m: the creation of ~$12.4m of value is the heart of this deal, and it's why developing beats buying.

The $12.4m gap is a 40% profit on cost. Even in a bad scenario (income 7.5% lower, buyers paying less), the profit is still ~15% on cost — the deal has room to be wrong and still work.

4. How the money flows over eight years

Investors' money goes in during the two build years (gold bars), the building pays modest income while it fills up, and the big payoff comes when it's sold or refinanced around year eight. Investors earn about 21% per year on this pattern; that's what makes the raise sellable.

Gold = money invested during construction. Green = money coming back. Funded by outside equity ($14.0m) plus a bank loan ($17.2m). Not our capital.

Our side of the table

What the deal pays us for creating it

We are the sponsors: we find the deal, secure the land, get the permits, raise the money and run the development. For that, the standard structure pays us two ways. A development fee of 3% of the budget during the build, and a promote at the end: investors first earn a 10% yearly return on their money, then every profit dollar above that is split 80% to them, 20% to us.

The decision on the table

All of this starts with $50k

What the $50k does: it secures a 6–12 month purchase option or exclusivity on the land. Whoever controls this plot controls the deal; without it we have a nice analysis of someone else's opportunity.

What happens next: with the land locked, we verify everything that matters at low cost: clean title, the building size the city will approve, and a professional cost estimate. Those studies (roughly $300–500k) can be funded by a seed round from early investors, on terms that favor whoever moved first: us. Only after everything checks out does the $14m raise happen, from outside money.

What we risk: the $50k, and honestly that's it. If the checks kill the deal, we walk away and the option lapses. If they confirm it, we hold the keys to a project whose sponsor economics are worth $4.2m in the base case. That's the bet: $50k for the right to create a landmark.
